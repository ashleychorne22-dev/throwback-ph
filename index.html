Welcome to our time machine.

This phone-friendly museum is for students, young adults and anyone curious about Filipino nostalgia. Each of its three galleries has a 1990–2000 room and a present-day room. Read the short exhibit labels, explore the images and compare what changed—and what didn’t.

Lobby feature · Timeline

A decade that still plays on.

1990

OPM, rock, radio and variety TV shape everyday pop culture.

1991

Mt. Pinatubo erupts, marking Philippine life beyond entertainment.

1995

Republic Act 7925 establishes a public telecommunications policy.

1998

Actor Joseph Estrada is elected president, exemplifying showbiz-politics links.

2000

CDs, early texting and emerging music TV bridge decades.

Today

P-pop, streaming, short videos and online communities lead new participation.

Then vs. Now hall · Six categories

Different screens.
Familiar stories.

The museum follows six themes from its presentation: social, economic, political, cultural, environmental and other influences. These are interpretive comparisons—not claims that every Filipino had the same experience.

☺

Social

THEN · 1990–2000

Mass, scheduled and face-to-face: malls, cinemas, karaoke and early texts.

NOW · PRESENT

On-demand fan communities, group chats and hybrid concerts.

WHAT STAYED / CHANGED

The need to belong stays; the gathering place changes.

₱

Economic

THEN · 1990–2000

Mall boom, cassette/CD sales, network advertising and record labels.

NOW · PRESENT

Streaming, paid creators, mobile wallets and ticketed concerts.

WHAT STAYED / CHANGED

The star system remains, but more individuals can now produce and earn.

✦

Political

THEN · 1990–2000

Reestablished democratic expression and visible celebrity-to-politics connections.

NOW · PRESENT

Influencer endorsements, online advocacy and disinformation.

WHAT STAYED / CHANGED

Celebrity influence carries forward, now amplified by platforms.

♫

Cultural

THEN · 1990–2000

OPM, Pinoy rock, Taglish, imported telenovelas and localized global music.

NOW · PRESENT

P-pop, K-drama, streaming services, TikTok and 1990s nostalgia.

WHAT STAYED / CHANGED

Filipinos keep borrowing influences and making them their own.

◌

Environmental

THEN · 1990–2000

The 1991 Pinatubo eruption was a major event, though nature was often a backdrop in entertainment.

NOW · PRESENT

Climate awareness, relief concerts, conversations on e-waste and data centers.

WHAT STAYED / CHANGED

Environmental stories are more visible, but digital systems create new impacts.

✳

Others

THEN · 1990–2000

Rap and grunge localized; celebrity styles traveled through broadcast media.

NOW · PRESENT

K-pop influence, ukay-ukay, Gen Z slang and Filipino artists overseas.

WHAT STAYED / CHANGED

Global exchange is quicker, two-way and more participatory.

The big takeaway: the 1990s gave today’s pop culture its themes of identity, emotion and community. Technology made the present faster, more participatory and more global.

About the museum · Sources & credits

Made to remember.
Made to learn.

Throwback PH turns the provided Milestone 2 presentation into a school-project digital museum. All 38 exhibit cards now show actual publicly available images via online Wikimedia Commons image links. Every image has its own source and credit, and images require an internet connection.

Our museum idea

We look at Philippine popular culture through feelings, performance and technology, comparing 1990–2000 with the present. The six comparison themes give social, economic, political, cultural and environmental context. Captions are original educational summaries based on the presentation, with added context distinguished in the references.

Image use and transparency

The museum uses real public photographs, stills, screenshots and posters rather than locally bundled illustrations. All images load online from Wikimedia Commons and link to individual attribution and licensing pages.

Some past-era rooms use newer photos of historical artists, or examples photographed after 2000. Other topic images are representative global examples rather than documents of Philippine history. These differences are identified in each exhibit label. A website being used for school does not automatically remove licensing or attribution requirements.
